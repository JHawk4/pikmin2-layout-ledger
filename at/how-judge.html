How the judge works

The judge looks at a sublevel's layout (where the ship, the treasures, the enemies and the hole are) and estimates how many seconds a good runner needs to collect every treasure on it and leave. It never watches the run: it only sees the layout, so it can say how kind the layout was, apart from how well it was played.

  • Estimate: the time to walk the route, plus the time Pikmin spend carrying each treasure back to the ship, plus extra time for enemies near a carry path and for gates in the way, plus a fixed cost per treasure.
  • Missing treasures and purple flowers: a quirk of the spawning sometimes leaves a treasure out of the layout, or fewer purple flowers than usual where runners pick purples. The judge adds 300 s when a treasure is missing (as CaveGen's All Treasures judge does) and 10 s per missing purple flower (CaveGen: 60 s; see below).
  • Layout luck: the same estimate for 1000 random layouts of the sublevel. This layout against the median one is its luck (negative = faster), and the share of random layouts it beats is its percentile.
  • Runner: the real time from the video minus what the judge says the layout was worth. What is left is the runner's own doing.

Across 219 sublevel plays on this site, one second the judge predicts is worth 0.93 real seconds (1 would be perfect), and its estimates correlate 0.39 with the real times. See Judge accuracy for the checks.

How it was made and checked

It is CaveGen's All Treasures judge, rebuilt in CaveGen3, with the Japanese release's treasure weights for Japanese runs. Its parts are weighted as the Pay Off Debt judge's, which were fitted on that category's real times: walking ×1.22, carrying ×0.78, hazards ×0.60, gates ×0.58. On the All Treasures runs here those weights come out right (a predicted second is worth about one real second), while CaveGen's own weights overstate layout differences by about a fifth. A fit on these runs alone could not do better: with a handful of runs, every reasonable set of weights explains about as much.

The missing-treasure cost could not be checked yet: none of these runs met a missing treasure. Missing purple flowers are common on a few sublevels (about a third of random BK 4 and FC 4 layouts, two thirds of SR 5), but the only two plays here that met them, both on SR 5, lost about 5 s (± 10 s), not a minute; with 60 s the judge overstated layout differences by 43%. So a missing purple counts 10 s until more runs show where purples matter more.

The comparison works within each sublevel and each runner, so it asks only: when the same runner meets a different layout of the same sublevel, does the time move as the judge says?

What it can't see
  • Execution: deaths, misplays, lucky enemy behaviour, how well a runner manages Pikmin.
  • Route habits and tricks the base judge doesn't model.
  • It is an estimate. Single sublevels can be off by several seconds; totals over a run are much steadier.
