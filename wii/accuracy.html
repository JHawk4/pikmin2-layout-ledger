Does the judge predict real times?

Each sublevel's real time is measured from the video: from its entry screen to the next sublevel's entry screen (a cave's last sublevel runs to the cave's results screen). Both the judge's estimate and the real time are compared within the same sublevel across runs, after removing each runner's overall pace. For Pay Off Debt the outcome counts pokos too, at two pokos per second; for other categories, plays where the runner collected a different number of treasures than most runs of that sublevel are left out. Only seeds read at high confidence are used: a misread seed is a wrong layout, so its judge score would be noise. Hover a point for its seed and pokos; click it to open the video there.

The judge is right on average: a layout it calls 10 s faster really is about 10 s faster. It explains about 45% of the differences between runs on the same sublevel; the rest is the runners.

Over 160 sublevel plays (46 sublevels seen in at least two runs), each second the judge predicts should be worth one second of net time.

The numbers

The slope is what a predicted second is actually worth (1 = calibrated, below 1 = the judge overstates differences between layouts); R² is the share of the runs' differences the judge explains taking its seconds at face value. The judge is CaveGen's Pay Off Debt judge, with the parts and weights refit on the GameCube Pay Off Debt runs (see that section's judge page), and its scale refit here on these Wii runs. Wii and Wii U loads are shorter, and on the Wii U many sublevel titles are cut short or skipped, so fewer sublevels have a seed than on GameCube.

Is the judge equally right everywhere?

No sign that the judge is better or worse for any cave, runner or layout size.

Show the checks

Each chart walks through the same plays in a different order, adding up prediction² along the bottom and prediction × actual up the side, so the slope of the walk so far is the regression slope over those plays. If the judge is off by the same proportion everywhere, the walk follows the dashed line and stays inside the grey 95% band. A bend that leaves the band shows where the slope differs: for example, a steeper end in "size" would mean big predicted swings are more reliable than small ones. The test allows each play its own noise level. Under each chart are the sort's values; drag across a chart to see the slope of just that stretch.
